Gallery — an exhibition in rooms

Frames from my world.

Pencil lines from the pocket sketchbook, a stone road into Chitlang, diaries from Jhapa and Ilam, café corners, lab notes from the robot bench, a classroom of researchers — life between the terminal and the mountains. Walk through slowly; every frame opens.

Admit one — free, always. Ten rooms · – frames
Open daily · Kathmandu, नेपाल
Curator: the guy with the pencil

१Room one

The sketchbook, pinned to the wall.

Graphite, iPad, whatever's near. Most of these were drawn in stolen minutes — between deploys, at a resort railing, in the office art hour with friends whose work hangs here too.

२Room two

Chitlang, on foot.

A stone road, a blue roof, a tree that caught the sun at exactly the right moment, and friends walking ahead. Shot as it came — one roll, no retakes.

३Room three

The Jhapa diary.

Judging at the WCBT hackathon, a run fest at dawn, and more second coffees than I'll admit.

४Room four

Ilam, above the clouds.

Kanyam's tea rows, a horseman and his horse, and the team sitting on a hill with nothing to fix.

५Room five

Cafés & corners.

Where the book comes out of the bag. The walls usually have better advice than I do.

६Room six

Lab notes from the robot bench.

Before the robot moves, the maths moves on paper. Encoders, wheels, radii — by hand first.

७Room seven

The classroom, this time for researchers.

AI for Research at Prakriti Resource Centre — the latest training we ran. Laptops open, prompts on the big screen, and a room full of people who ask better questions than most engineers.

today's lesson:

1. ask the model

2. check the source

3. you write the paper

— teach what you just learned
८Room eight

Contact sheet, mostly me.

Black and white until you look closer. The red circle marks the keeper.

Circled: the medal collection. Every one of them came with a sunrise.

९Room nine

Travel, hung salon-style.

Too many to label, too good to leave in the phone. Nepal, mostly — ridge after ridge.

१०Room ten

Moving pictures.

Lights down. Hover to play, tap for sound.

It is the spectator, and not life, that art really mirrors.
