What the books left behind.

Lines I underlined at 2 a.m., pages with folded corners, sentences that survived the highlighter. Not reviews — just the fragments that keep arguing in my head, and why they stayed.

“When we are no longer able to change a situation, we are challenged to change ourselves.”
Viktor E. Frankl — Man's Search for Meaning
Man's Search for MeaningViktor E. Frankl
“Everything can be taken from a man but one thing: the last of the human freedoms — to choose one's attitude in any given set of circumstances.”
Why it stayedA concentration-camp survivor writing about freedom of attitude rearranged how I think about hard weeks. There is always one variable left under my control.
MeditationsMarcus Aurelius
“The impediment to action advances action. What stands in the way becomes the way.”
Why it stayedEvery production incident is this sentence with a timestamp. The blocker isn't blocking the work — it is the work.
AntifragileNassim Nicholas Taleb
“Wind extinguishes a candle and energizes fire.”
Why it stayedSame chaos, two outcomes — the difference is what you're made of. I want to be the thing that gets stronger when the plan breaks.
The Psychology of MoneyMorgan Housel
“Wealth is what you don't see.”
Why it stayedThe loudest purchases are usually the quietest bank accounts. Saved me from confusing income with wealth — in money and in attention.
Zero to OnePeter Thiel
“Every moment in business happens only once.”
Why it stayedThe next Bill Gates won't build an operating system, and the next NAXA won't build another geoportal. Copying removes the value; the moment is the moat.
Notes from UndergroundFyodor Dostoevsky
“I am a sick man… I am a spiteful man. I am an unattractive man. I believe my liver is diseased.”
Why it stayedThe most honest opening line in literature. Everyone has an underground man — mine talks mostly at 2 a.m., right after I say I'll sleep early.
The Picture of Dorian GrayOscar Wilde
“The only way to get rid of a temptation is to yield to it.”
Why it stayedBeautiful, logical, and completely wrong — which is exactly why it's terrifying. Wilde writes warnings that sound like invitations.
Beyond Good and EvilFriedrich Nietzsche
“He who fights with monsters should see to it that he himself does not become a monster.”
Why it stayedWritten for anyone who has fought bad process with harsher process, or answered a rude code review with a ruder one. The abyss also reads pull requests.
Socrates ExpressEric Weiner
“The unexamined life may not be worth living, but the over-examined life is no picnic either.”
Why it stayedPhilosophy as a practical toolkit, not a museum. Bonus memory: I met Eric Weiner at Boudha — told him this book rode every bus in Kathmandu with me.
The 80/20 PrincipleRichard Koch
“A minority of causes, inputs, or effort usually lead to a majority of the results, outputs, or rewards.”
Why it stayedTwenty percent of the code causes eighty percent of the incidents — I've lived this table of contents. Profile before you optimise, in systems and in weeks.
Million Dollar WeekendNoah Kagan
“Validation is finding three customers in forty-eight hours who will give you money for your idea.”
Why it stayedThe antidote to building in secret for six months. Ask first, build second — the same reason I ship an ugly working demo before writing a single design doc.
Screw Business as UsualRichard Branson
“Screw it, let's do it.”
Why it stayedTwo words that beat a hundred planning meetings. I watched this exact energy turn hackathon wins into an actual company — my juniors didn't wait for permission, and neither should anyone.
The Geography of BlissEric Weiner
“Happiness is not a noun or verb. It's a conjunction. Connective tissue.”
Why it stayedA grump travelling the world to find happy places, and the finding is always the same: people. Explains why my best work days involve a full room, and why I met this very author at Boudha and talked his ear off.
21 Lessons for the 21st CenturyYuval Noah Harari
“In a world deluged by irrelevant information, clarity is power.”
Why it stayedCould be the tagline for every data pipeline I've built. Most systems don't fail from missing data — they drown in the irrelevant kind.

Reading is a conversation.

I read the way I debug — slowly, with a pen. A book only finishes when something from it survives into how I think on Monday morning. These are the survivors: lines copied into notebooks, phone notes, and eventually into code reviews, standups, and 2 a.m. decisions.

Frankl handed me the why before the how. Taleb taught me to stop resenting volatility. Housel separated money from noise, Harari separated signal from flood. Dostoevsky, honestly, just keeps me company.

If one of these lines starts an argument in your head too — good. That argument is the point. Bring it to me over coffee; the best conversations start in the margins.
